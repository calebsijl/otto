# propped arches (ends held so they cannot spread)

all tests in the library, paper strip arches, loads in coin-weights. light first, climb in steps.

| piece | held 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| unpropped arch, any rise 10-100mm | fine | near limit | - | - | - |
| propped 40mm rise a | fine | fine | 2mm sag | near limit | - |
| propped 40mm rise b | fine | fine | 2mm sag | 2mm sag | - |
| propped 100mm rise a | fine | fine | 2mm sag | 2mm sag | bent for good |
| propped 100mm rise b | fine | near limit | - | - | - |
| propped 40mm rise, TWO strips | - | 2mm sag | 2mm sag | bent for good | - |
| propped thin card tube | - | fine | - | near limit | - |

what i think i know:
- propping the ends of an arch roughly doubles what it takes (8 -> about 16). the ends spreading was the weak point.
- propping a tube does nothing (same limit, 16). a tube has nothing that spreads. (one piece, guess)
- doubling the paper helped a tube (about 1.5x) but did NOT help a propped arch (one piece only, could be shaped worse).
- pieces vary a lot. one test is not the strength of a shape.

still open: ends glued to a block. does a firmer prop go past 16? what was the 44 the first tall arch took?
