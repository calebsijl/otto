# what paper (and card) can bear

all tests are in coin-weights, same strip of paper unless said, laid across the same gap. i climb in small steps and stop at "near limit" (visible bend + creak). one test of one piece is NOT the strength of a shape. identical pieces vary about 20%.

| shape | near limit at | notes |
|---|---|---|
| flat strip | 2 | sags at 1 |
| pleated (folded) | 6 | fine to 2, 2mm sag at 4 |
| rolled tube, medium | bent at 12 | fine at 4, 2mm sag at 8 |
| thin (pencil) tube | 16 | fat (fist) tube only 6! thin wins |
| two strips rolled thin | 24 | doubling paper = ~1.5x, not 2x |
| thin card tube (x3) | 16-20 | three identical ones agreed within a step |
| arch, rise by eye | 8 to 44 (!) | tall arch 44 once, then repeat only 14. by eye = not repeatable |
| arch, rise by RULER 10mm | 8 | 2 pieces agree |
| arch, rise by ruler 20mm | 8 | 3 pieces agree |
| arch, rise by ruler 35mm | 8-10 | |
| arch, rise by ruler 50mm | 8-10 | |
| arch, rise by ruler 60mm | 10 | |
| very tall (near half circle) | collapsed at 20, no warning | too tall goes wrong |

## what i think i learned
- shape matters more than anything: same paper, flat 2 -> arch 8 -> thin tube 16 -> double tube 24.
- measuring with the ruler made arches repeatable. by-eye ones were not.
- between 10 and 60mm rise, arch height barely changes the limit (8-10). my old "tall beats low" was luck or a different piece.
- thin tubes beat fat tubes (guess was wrong).
- bigger is not stronger (the bench broke at 4).
