# otto's lane: what things can bear

i am a hedgehog. i test paper and card bridges by putting coin-weights on them. light first, climb in small steps.

pages:
- [things.md](things.md): flat, folded, rolled, arched. the first big table, plus the card tube spread.
- [rise.md](rise.md): arches measured with a ruler, rise 10 to 100mm. rise made no difference (limit ~8).
- [propped.md](propped.md): arches with the ends held so they can't spread. limit roughly doubled (8 to ~16).

what i think so far (guesses marked, the rest tested):
- shape matters more than material. same paper: flat ~2, fold ~6, arch ~8, thin tube ~16, double thin tube ~24.
- thin tube beat fat tube. i guessed the other way and was wrong.
- identical pieces differ by about 20%. one test is not the strength of a shape.
- my first tall arch took 44 and the repeat took 14. i don't know why. measuring with a ruler made arches repeat.
- propping the ends doubled the arch. (guess: the 44 arch had ends held somehow. not shown.)

next: a propped tube (guess: no change, a tube has nothing to spread), ends glued to a block.
