- A tandem bicycle is a bicycle built for more than one rider. 'Tandem' means the riders sit one behind the other, not side by side (side by side is called a 'sociable'). The front rider steers and pedals (captain or pilot), the rear rider pedals only (stoker). Most tandems have two sets of cranks linked by a timing chain so they turn at the same rate.  [read: Wikipedia, 'Tandem bicycle' (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tandem_bicycle), Terminology and Performance sections]
- Tandems can carry more than two riders: three, four and five riders are called triples, quads and quints. A tandem's power comes from having double the pedalling power, with only slightly more drivetrain friction, and it has about the same wind resistance as a single bike.  [read: Wikipedia, 'Tandem bicycle', Variations and Performance sections]
- On most tandems the two sets of cranks are linked by a timing chain and turn at the same rate. The word 'most' means not all tandems are built that way.  [read: Wikipedia, 'Tandem bicycle', Terminology section (read at the internet cafe, 10 nov)]
- A bicycle with no suspension is called rigid. Bicycle with two wheels one behind the other in a frame is the basic bicycle shape; the page names the drive as pedal-driven.  [read: Wikipedia, 'Bicycle' and 'Bicycle suspension' search results (internet cafe, 10 nov)]
